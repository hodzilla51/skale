## 1. Task shape

The problem concerns X, who loses stable employment and housing, later loses resident registration after ceasing to live at an NPO shelter, is denied public assistance because Y City refuses to treat internet cafes or building eaves as a `居住地` or `現在地`, and is also unable to vote after being removed from the electoral register.

The problem therefore contains two main constitutional branches:

1. real protection of subsistence / public assistance;
2. effective exercise of the right to vote.

The examinee must make X's constitutional claims and then state an independent view while anticipating the defendant's counterarguments.

## 2. Examiner-required analytical operations

### O1 — Partition the welfare and voting branches

The two deprivations arise through different legal structures and require separate analysis.

### O2 — Identify the exact legal target instead of attacking everything

For public assistance, the central problem is not facial invalidity of the Public Assistance Act. The statute already uses `居住地` and `現在地`; the issue is Y City's interpretation and application / operation of those terms.

For voting, the central structural problem is that election law contains no mechanism allowing a person without an address to exercise the vote. That is naturally analyzed as a legislative-omission problem rather than merely as invalidity of one isolated provision or invalidity of the resident-registration deletion itself.

### O3 — Determine the protected interest before selecting a test

The public-assistance branch concerns Article 25 subsistence protection as concretized through the Public Assistance Act.

The voting branch concerns the effective exercise of the vote itself, not merely expression by another name. Its importance must be grounded in democratic constitutional structure, including popular sovereignty and representative government.

### O4 — Use constitutional purpose to interpret an existing statute

Because the Public Assistance Act already concretizes Article 25 and expressly covers persons with a `現在地`, the answer should interpret and apply the statute in light of its subsistence-protection purpose.

The examiner signals reject skipping the actual statutory text and jumping directly from X's hardship to an abstract Article 25 conclusion.

### O5 — Suppress the wrong doctrinal branches

The welfare issue is not primarily:

- a legislative-discretion problem;
- a dispute over what quantitative level counts as `最低限度`;
- a facial challenge to the Public Assistance Act;
- a long abstract debate over the legal character of Article 25;
- a generic purpose-means review of Y City's interpretation detached from the statute.

The problem asks whether Y City's interpretation / operation is legally sustainable in light of the statute and constitutional purpose.

### O6 — Analyze regional differential treatment with institutional context

Another municipality treats the same type of location as `居住地` or `現在地`, while Y City does not.

This can raise equality concerns, but the analysis must account for the relationship between equal protection, the national statutory purpose of public assistance, and local autonomy. The examiner materials signal the Supreme Court's 1958 local-ordinance precedent and require attention to the differences between that precedent and this case.

### O7 — Use the controlling voting-right precedent rather than a generic strictness label

The 2005 overseas-voting-rights Grand Bench judgment supplies the key rule for restrictions on the exercise of voting rights: restriction requires an unavoidable reason, tied to practical impossibility or extreme difficulty of allowing the vote while preserving electoral fairness.

The answer must concretely examine what fairness problems arise if persons without addresses vote and whether workable alternatives exist.

### O8 — Separate substantive unconstitutionality of legislative omission from State Redress Act illegality

The absence of a voting mechanism raises a substantive constitutional problem. A damages claim based on legislative omission raises a separate State Redress Act illegality question.

The examiner commentary specifically criticizes answers that fail to distinguish these two questions.

### O9 — Use the seven-year petition fact as a notice / persistence fact

NPOs had sought statutory amendment seven years before the election in question. That fact matters to the State Redress analysis because it bears on legislative awareness and the time available to respond; it is not merely background detail.

### O10 — Convert poverty / housing facts into legal reasons rather than sympathy

X lost regular employment, housing, stable resident registration, health-insurance coverage, and practical access to medical treatment despite continued efforts to work.

These facts matter because they show the real-world consequence of Y City's interpretation and the connection between statutory access and protection of bare subsistence. They should not be used merely as emotional description.

### O11 — Evaluate Y City's actual reasons

Y City's image concerns and fiscal concerns are supplied as concrete reasons for its restrictive operation. The answer must test their legal relevance against the statutory and constitutional structure rather than merely asserting `裁量`.

### O12 — Build the strongest opposing position efficiently

As in earlier years, the defendant's strongest points should be identified and integrated into the independent analysis. The official materials again reject strategic three-tier standard shopping in which each party mechanically receives a different level of scrutiny.

## 4. Strong grading-failure signals

### F1 — Facial challenge to the Public Assistance Act

The official commentary treats this as the wrong target; the central welfare problem is interpretation / application of existing statutory language.

### F2 — Abstract Article 25 theory without reading the statute

Long discussion of abstract-right / programmatic theories is not a substitute for interpreting the concrete provisions that already implement subsistence protection.

### F3 — Generic administrative discretion

Saying only that the city has broad discretion does not address the statutory text, purpose, or concrete reasons.

### F4 — Template purpose-means review everywhere

The grading materials again criticize answers that reach automatically for a generic review-standard template rather than identifying the proper legal operation.

### F5 — Mechanical rule/application dualism

Knowing the labels `法令違憲` and `適用違憲` is not enough. The examiner explicitly notes that both need not exist in every branch.

### F6 — Voting-right analysis derived from expression doctrine

The importance of voting should be grounded in voting's own constitutional position, including popular sovereignty and representative democracy, rather than borrowed mechanically from freedom-of-expression theory.

### F7 — Missing the 2005 overseas-voting precedent

The examiner materials treat this as a basic and important controlling precedent for both voting restriction and legislative omission.

### F8 — Treating legislative omission and damages illegality as the same question

Substantive constitutional invalidity and State Redress Act illegality require separate treatment.

### F9 — Citing precedent without comparing facts

Both the regional-treatment precedent and the overseas-voting precedent must be used with attention to factual differences, including the seven-year petition and the actual feasibility of alternative voting arrangements.

### F10 — Copying facts without explaining their legal effect

The teacher signals again require individual, concrete fact evaluation.

## 5. Authorities / doctrinal anchors signaled

- Constitution Article 25 — right to maintain minimum standards of wholesome and cultured living / subsistence protection
- Public Assistance Act, especially Article 19 `居住地` / `現在地`
- equality principle in relation to differing local treatment
- local autonomy context
- Supreme Court Grand Bench, 1958-10-15, Tokyo prostitution-control ordinance case, as an anchor concerning differing local treatment
- constitutional voting right / effective exercise of voting rights
- popular sovereignty and representative democracy as reasons for the importance of voting
- Supreme Court Grand Bench, 2005-09-14, overseas-voting-rights case
- legislative omission
- State Redress Act illegality for legislative omission, distinguished from substantive unconstitutionality
- rule-level versus application / disposition-level constitutional review

This is not a complete constitutional-law doctrinal inventory.

## 6. Candidate year-level procedure

```text
partition welfare and voting claims
→ identify exact legal target for each
→ identify protected interest and legal relationship
→ choose the correct legal operation, not merely a scrutiny label
→ welfare: interpret / apply existing statute through Article 25 purpose
→ voting: analyze missing mechanism as legislative omission under controlling precedent
→ convert concrete facts into legal reasons
→ test equality / locality, electoral fairness, alternatives, and notice where relevant
→ separate substantive unconstitutionality from damages illegality
→ suppress tempting but non-fitting branches
→ integrate defendant's strongest rationale
→ independent conclusion
```

## 7. Particularly important SKALE observations

### 7.1 `法令違憲 vs 適用違憲` is a router, not a mandatory pair

2010 strongly confirms that learning the distinction is useful only if the learner can decide which target actually exists. A model that always generates both branches is wrong.

### 7.2 The correct backend may be statutory interpretation rather than constitutional scrutiny

The public-assistance branch is a strong example where the constitutional value operates through interpretation and application of an already-existing statutory scheme. `Pick a scrutiny test` is therefore too narrow as a universal reasoning rule.

### 7.3 Legislative omission is a distinct scheme-level failure mode

The voting branch is not merely `this provision is too restrictive`; the defect can be the absence of a mechanism needed to make a right practically exercisable.

### 7.4 Remedies / consequences may contain separate legal tests

Even after constitutional invalidity is analyzed, damages liability for legislative omission is a distinct legal question. This supports keeping claim / consequence routing visible rather than collapsing everything into one merits conclusion.

### 7.5 Fact-to-reason conversion remains central

The seven-year petition, one-district fact, alternative local operation, health consequences, and City's stated motives are useful only when converted into the legal reason they change.
